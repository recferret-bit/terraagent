TerraAgent whitepaper · web3 part · draft, 2026-11-02

Every actor is a NEAR account. Gameplay never waits for the chain.

The game server simulates the colony. NEAR holds what players own and settles what they trade; only a trade's own settlement waits for a block. This page shows how each on-chain feature works, why it is built that way, and what is real on demo day.

00 · overview

Three layers, one owner of each truth

The simulation decides what happened. NEAR records who owns what. Every chain write leaves the server through one outbox and one relayer, so the client never signs and players never pay gas.

F5 · accounts and keys

One root, a named account for every actor

Real after Nov 10

Players, colonies and NPC traders each get a readable sub-account under terraagent.near. Players never see a seed phrase; the app holds no root key, and only the relayer and minter keys are online.

F5 · login

Play first, sign up when it is worth saving

Real after Nov 10NEAR Auth: proposed

A guest founds a colony with no account. Saving progress turns a social login into a named NEAR account, and each existing colony gets its own sub-account. If NEAR Auth is not ready, the fallback is server-created custodial accounts.

F5 · gasless, crash-safe writes

A chain write is a database row first

Outbox builtRelayer real after Nov 10

The game change and its chain write commit in one database transaction. If the server dies halfway, the row is claimed again and the same operation id returns the stored receipt, so nothing is minted or paid twice.

F1 · cargo tokens

The Trade Hub is the only door

Real after Nov 10

Cargo becomes a token only when a colony exports simulated stock through its Trade Hub, and stops being one when it comes back. Production sets supply, so there is no faucet. One NEP-245 contract holds all ten cargo types.

F2 · markets on NEAR Intents

Every trade is one batch that sums to zero

Real after Nov 10

Orders are matched in the game server and settled on intents.near, so we deploy no exchange contract. Each fill is a batch of signed balance changes (maker, taker, fee legs). The verifier checks that every token sums to zero and applies all of it or none of it.

F3 · Orbital Guild traders

A merchant that is always there, with a price you can read

Mock on Nov 14AI strategy: proposed

Two NPC traders, Kestrel and Osprey, have their own accounts and inventories. They quote both sides of every pair from one published rule, so a quiet planet still has a market, and their prices react to what players sell.

F4 · the colony director

The colony trades while you sleep, inside limits you set

Mock on Nov 14Allowance unit: open

While the player is away, the director sells surplus and buys shortages with its own revocable key. The game server checks every order twice before anything is signed. The spending limit is a server rule, not a property of the key.

API · outside AI agents

Agents as players: where does the limit live?

A: proposedB: open, probe first

Any external AI agent could play a colony through the same commands a player uses. There are two ways to hold its keys and limits, and they differ in who enforces the policy.

F6 · colony deed, Settled, staking

A deed that grows with the colony

Deed: mock on Nov 14Settled, staking: later

Each colony has one dynamic NFT (NEP-171) that records its growth and can be shared as a card. At the end, a colony that launches its rocket becomes Settled, and its deed lets the player stake their own NEAR, with no in-game power.

F7 · planet treasury

Fees fund the planet, colonies vote on the spend

Mock on Nov 14

A fifth of the platform fee (D-44) goes to the planet's DAO; the stall owner keeps the whole stall fee. Its members are the colony accounts, and they vote on projects that change the simulation for everyone, with no patch needed.

F8 · bounties

The reward is locked before the hunt starts

Mock on Nov 14

A player posts a reward for a target, such as clearing a nest or defending an area. The reward moves into escrow at once, so the claimer is paid by the contract, not by the poster's goodwill.

IoC · ports and adapters

Going real is a config change, not a rewrite

Built (W00, on main)

Every NEAR feature talks to an abstract port. One environment variable per port picks its adapter at boot, and the same contract test suite runs against the mock and the real chain. This part already exists.

Nov 14 · what is real

One check on Nov 10 decides what is real

Real after Nov 10Mock

If the core game passes the Nov 10 check, login, cargo and markets switch to real NEAR, one at a time and in that order. If it does not, the demo runs on mocks and says so. Everything else stays mock for the hackathon.
